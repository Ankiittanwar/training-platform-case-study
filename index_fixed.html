How I built the training platform a growing team actually needed.

Six weeks. A brief that came out of six real conversations. A production web application that gave the team back most of the time they were losing to manual onboarding.

The Problem

Every new hire was quietly costing the team a full day of senior time.

The team was growing fast. Every person who joined needed to learn the product catalogue, the ordering system, the client protocols, the internal tools. All of it was being taught the way it had always been taught. One person walking another person through everything, over two weeks, in whatever way that first person happened to remember it.

10+ hrs

Time per new hire

Spread across a fortnight of one to one sessions. Nobody had ever added it up because nobody had time to.

Two truths

Consistency drift

Two hires trained by two people got two slightly different versions of correct. Small drifts, every time.

Zero

Manager visibility

"Did Sarah finish her training?" got answered with "I think so." No dashboard. No signal. No trail.

Off the shelf platforms had been tried twice already. Docebo was too expensive for the team size. Google Classroom felt built for schools and did not fit how retail staff actually work. What the team had ended up with was a folder in Google Drive and a shared spreadsheet that tracked nothing meaningfully.

Three Team Leads
"What do new hires actually need to know in their first week?"
Click to read the answer
The list was much shorter than what was being taught. Roughly 60 percent of onboarding time went to things a new hire would pick up naturally in month one anyway. The other 40 percent was the actual critical knowledge. And no one had ever written that part down.
Two Recent Hires
"What confused you? What did you have to ask three times?"
Click to read the answer
Both said the same thing without me prompting. There was no way to go back and check something later. Everything happened verbally. Two weeks in they still had questions but felt embarrassed to keep asking. What they needed was a reference book, not a training session.
The Founder
"What does 'onboarded well' actually mean to you?"
Click to read the answer
He said "I want to know they can walk the floor on day three without shadowing someone the whole time." That answer changed the whole product. Success was not test scores. Success was independence.
Myself, After The Six Calls
"What are people actually going to use this on?"
Click to read the answer
Not a laptop. Warehouse and floor staff work shifts. Training would happen on lunch breaks, before shifts, on the commute home. This had to feel like a phone app. Not a website that shrinks down to fit a screen.
i.
The content already existed

The problem was not writing content. The problem was that the content lived in twelve WhatsApp threads and no one place.

ii.
Mobile first, not responsive

More than 80 percent of use would happen on a phone. Every layout choice had to start there and work backwards.

iii.
Completion, not analytics

"Did Sarah finish" was the whole question. What was needed was a dashboard, not a metrics warehouse.

What Comes Next

Version one is live. This is version two.

The roadmap the team and I are working through now.

Building
AI assisted module generation

Feed in an existing SOP document, get back a draft module with sections, key points, and knowledge checks. Cuts content creation time by roughly 60 percent.

Planned
Retention testing

Re quiz users at 30, 60, and 90 days. The real question is not "did they finish" but "do they still know it three months later."

Planned
Multilingual support

Modules rendered in the reader's native language, through translated content or on demand LLM translation.

Backlog
Notifications

Managers get pinged when a new hire completes a module. Assigned modules that stay untouched get a nudge.
